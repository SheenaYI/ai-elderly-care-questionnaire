# Direction contract — 居家照护研究工作簿

**Mode:** Operate · **Build path:** code-led · **Roll:** degraded (no external challengers were available).

## Why this exists

The page makes a DCE trade-off experiential without changing the event itself: participants see the same scripted suspected-fall event but can compare the information recipients and exposure that each configuration permits.

## Chosen system

The visual system is a refined home-care research workbook: warm ivory paper-like surfaces, deep evergreen for committed selections and system trust, misty sage for neutral structure, and a precise amber wash to mark only the experimental attributes that differ. It deliberately avoids the default health-tech dashboard and any sci-fi control-room vocabulary.

## First viewport

The participant immediately sees the research task, the 1/12 progress context, the scenario framing, and the two equally weighted configurations. The choice is the page’s center of gravity; technology is explanatory evidence, not the product being sold.

## Information hierarchy

1. What is being chosen and why.
2. The two option cards and their exact three attributes.
3. A clearly separated affordance to experience a neutral scripted scenario.
4. A deliberate, confirmable research choice.

## Signature interaction and motion

Selecting a card produces a restrained border and radio transition. The scenario overlay uses one stateful run: calm monitoring → muted alert → received notification → configuration-specific disclosure. Motion communicates a transition only, respects reduced-motion preferences, and never changes the risk or speed implied by the simulation.

## Quality bar

The interface must remain legible for middle-aged and older participants: high-contrast Chinese copy, generous rows, 44px-or-larger touch controls, keyboard-operable dialogs, visible focus, no information that relies on colour alone, and structural mobile reflow.

## Seven grounded candidates considered

1. Community health consent packet — trusted and clear, but risks looking administrative.
2. Senior-care service record — domestically familiar, but can make the study feel like a sales brochure.
3. Ethics-review comparison board — rigorous, but overly academic for participants.
4. Household safety handbook — warm and concrete, but could over-emphasize hazard.
5. Shared-care notification ledger — exposes the mechanism clearly, but is too event-centric.
6. **Home-care research workbook** — balances neutral research clarity with a tangible, gentle care-system experience across quiet, choice, event, and disclosure states.
7. Digital polling ballot — direct and accessible, but undersells the simulation’s explanatory role.

## Pinned constraints carried from the brief

- Low-saturation deep green, grey-green, warm ivory, and restrained amber.
- Two equal A/B cards; differing attributes alone get a warm-amber treatment.
- The monitoring simulation may be darker and more technical, but must stay professional rather than game-like.
- No invented changes to accuracy, response speed, cost, or any attribute beyond the three supplied DCE attributes.
